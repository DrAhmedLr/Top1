# Research for the 21 previously uncited metrics

Research pass: 13 November 2026. This is an evidence review, not a deployed scoring change. The previous audit's “missing” label meant no verified source attached in TOP1, not that no scientific literature exists.

## Method and evidence boundaries

Searched original studies through PubMed/PMC and journal publishers, prioritizing test validity/reliability, population reference distributions, and clinical relevance. Searches combined exact metric names with normative values, validity, reliability, cohort and the original registry's candidate authors. Official guidance and study abstracts were used where full text was unavailable. This is a targeted research pass, not a systematic review or exhaustive search.

A relevant paper does not validate TOP1's mean, standard deviation, optimal target, score transformation, TOP% thresholds, estimated 1RM formula, or future success probabilities. A clinical risk marker is not an athletic performance rank. None of the sources below validates the existing universal worldwide Top 1% claims.

Full article text was directly accessible for the Adult-Fit broad-jump paper; the ASCA pull-up/dip abstract and the ApoB PubMed abstract were directly inspected. Other records were evaluated from retrievable abstracts/indexed publisher or PubMed text; several full-text opens returned access errors or bot challenges. Exact tables/protocols and extraction remain to be checked before implementing comparisons. The dips 2026 record in particular is a candidate based on indexed primary-source material; full text was blocked in this session.

## Evidence matrix

| Metric / app ID | Primary research located | What it supports | Fit, limitations and proposed use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strict push-ups / `pushups` | [Yang et al. 2019, JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2724778/), DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.8341 | Observational association between push-up capacity and subsequent cardiovascular events in 1,104 active male firefighters. | Occupational male cohort, not global norms or proof of benefit from increasing repetitions. Match cadence/depth/stopping rules. Use as contextual evidence; no conversion of 40 repetitions into Top 1%. |
| Eyes-closed single-leg balance / `single_leg_balance` | [Springer et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19839175/), DOI 10.1519/00139143-200704000-00003 | Age-specific reference performance and inter-rater reliability in 549 healthy adults; eyes-open and eyes-closed conditions. | Candidate age reference, not a representative world percentile distribution. Best/mean of three, stance and test ceiling need matching. Do not construct tail percentiles from means/SD alone. |
| Dips / `dips` | [Coyne et al., reliability of pull-up and dip maximal strength tests](https://www.strengthandconditioning.org/jasc-23-4/136-original-scientific-research-study-reliability-of-pull-up-and-dip-maximal-strength-tests); [2026 male collegiate gymnast trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12925195/) | ASCA: repeated 1RM tests in 15 male athletes. Gymnast study: bar-dip endurance assessed as repetitions in 60 seconds in a 30-man intervention study. | Partial match: 1RM and timed endurance differ from our untimed consecutive strict repetitions. The latter paper is an additional candidate pending full-text verification. No evidence here validates 25 repetitions as a population tier. |
| Standing broad jump / `broad_jump` | [Marin-Jimenez et al. 2024, Adult-Fit](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11369318/), DOI 10.1002/ejsc.12182 | Criterion-related validity and reliability in 410 adults; learning/familiarization materially affects repeated performance. | Good test-support source. Standardize takeoff, landing, trial selection and practice. Not a global percentile study. |
| Bench press / `bench_1rm` | [Kravitz et al. 2003, Prediction of 1 repetition maximum in high-school power lifters](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12580673/); [LeSuer et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1519/00124278-199711000-00001) | Direct 1RM testing and evaluation of submaximal prediction equations. Kravitz studied 18 elite male high-school powerlifters. | Supports a strength test/estimation research basis. Selected samples do not establish general-adult bodyweight-ratio norms. TOP1's specific formula needs equation-level validation. |
| Barbell squat / `squat_1rm` | [Kravitz et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12580673/); [LeSuer et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1519/00124278-199711000-00001) | Squat 1RM and prediction research. | Match depth/equipment; estimate is distinct from measured maximum. No global percentile calibration from these studies. |
| Deadlift / `deadlift_1rm` | [Kravitz et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12580673/); [LeSuer et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1519/00124278-199711000-00001) | Deadlift 1RM and prediction research. | Stance, equipment and lockout rules matter. No general-population ratio distribution validated here. |
| Weighted pull-up / `pullup_1rm` | [Coyne et al., ASCA original study](https://www.strengthandconditioning.org/jasc-23-4/136-original-scientific-research-study-reliability-of-pull-up-and-dip-maximal-strength-tests); [Johnson et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19387371/) | Reliable standardized maximal pull-up testing; strength/endurance relationships. Johnson studied 35 men and 23 women. | Distinguish total system mass from added load/bodyweight. Does not validate TOP1's multi-repetition extrapolation or universal added-load tiers. |
| 5 km run / `run_5k` | [Hurst & Board, Reliability of 5-km Running Performance in a Competitive Environment](https://doi.org/10.1080/1091367X.2016.1233421); [Intra-Subject Variability of 5 km Time Trial Performance, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5504586/) | Reproducibility of standardized time trials. | The 5 km evidence card cites Hurst and Board for test repeatability. Match course/treadmill, terrain and timing. Neither makes 20 minutes a global Top 1% threshold. |
| Zone 2 cycling power / `zone2_power` | [San-Millan & Brooks 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28623613/), Assessment of Metabolic Flexibility by Means of Measuring Blood Lactate, Fat, and Carbohydrate Oxidation Responses to Exercise | Exercise metabolic testing and differences between endurance athletes and less-fit individuals. | Physiological context only. “Zone 2” is ambiguous between zone systems. Define threshold, ergometer, steady-state protocol and power normalization before comparing W/kg. Does not validate 3.3 W/kg or any current tier. |
| Body fat / `body_fat` | [Kelly, Wilson & Heymsfield 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19753111/), DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0007038; [Li et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19812179/), DOI 10.3945/ajcn.2009.28141 | NHANES DXA reference distributions; the latter analyzes 6,559 men and 6,507 nonpregnant women. | Strong candidate for a fourth population-reference metric. US sample; age/sex/ethnicity and DXA processing matter. Consumer BIA/skinfolds are not interchangeable. A low percentile is not automatically a better health result. |
| Sleep efficiency / `sleep_efficiency` | [Ohayon et al. 2017, National Sleep Foundation's sleep quality recommendations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28346153/), DOI 10.1016/j.sleh.2016.11.006 | Expert consensus identifies sleep efficiency among sleep-continuity indicators. | Guidance, not an original population percentile distribution. Define denominator and device/diary/PSG method; wearable estimates differ. Does not support our 96% target as an elite percentile. |
| Circadian regularity / `circadian_regularity` | [Phillips et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-03171-4); [Windred et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37738616/) | Sleep Regularity Index development/use and prospective association with mortality. | Sleep regularity is a proxy for one aspect of timing consistency, not a direct circadian-phase measurement. Arbitrary wearable /100 values are not necessarily SRI. Define algorithm and input record before attaching this evidence to app scores. Observational association is not proof of causal benefit. |
| Reaction time / `reaction_time` | [Deary, Liewald & Nissan 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21287123/), DOI 10.3758/s13428-010-0024-1; [Der & Deary 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16594792/) | Computer-task validation in 150 adults aged 18–80; adult age/sex comparisons in a specified survey task. | Simple and choice reaction time must be separate. Device/input/display delay, practice, trial exclusions and summary statistic must match. No device-independent world percentile or 175 ms universal target. |
| Fasting glucose / `fasting_glucose` | [Diabetes Prevention Program Research Group 2002](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa012512) | Randomized prevention trial in 3,234 people with elevated fasting and post-load glucose. | Clinical relevance in a high-risk cohort. Clinical categories require current guidance and matching plasma/fasting units; not a sport score or proof that lower is always better. |
| Apolipoprotein B / `apob` | [Sniderman et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38700053/), DOI 10.1093/eurheartj/ehae258 | Prospective ASCVD-risk information in 293,876 UK Biobank adults, including discordance with cholesterol measures. | Supports biomarker relevance; no validation of TOP1's numerical target or fitness percentile. |
| hs-CRP / `hscrp` | [Ridker et al. 2008, JUPITER](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa0807646) | Randomized statin trial selecting participants with elevated hs-CRP. | Clinical-risk/intervention context, not a validation of ranking low hs-CRP as athletic ability. Does not establish that changing hs-CRP alone causes the observed benefit. |
| Fasting insulin / `fasting_insulin` | [Matthews et al. 1985, HOMA](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00280883) | A model using fasting insulin together with fasting glucose to estimate insulin resistance and beta-cell function, compared with independent methods. | Insulin alone is not HOMA. Assay/context variation and model uncertainty matter. No universal optimal insulin level or athlete percentile supported. |
| GGT / `ggt` | [Lee et al. 2003, CARDIA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12881453/) | Prospective association with incident diabetes and hypertension. | Observational clinical context. Not a causal training target, liver diagnosis in isolation, or world ranking. |
| Cystatin C / `cystatin_c` | [Inker et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22762315/), DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1114248 | Development/validation of kidney-filtration estimates using cystatin C and creatinine. | Supports use as a filtration marker in an equation/context. Raw cystatin C is not an athletic-performance percentile. |
| Homocysteine / `homocysteine` | [Lonn et al. 2006, HOPE-2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16531613/), DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa060900 | Randomized homocysteine-lowering intervention in 5,522 high-risk adults. | Important counterevidence: lowering the biomarker did not reduce the primary major cardiovascular-event outcome. No basis here for rewarding the lowest possible concentration as a health improvement. |

## Product implications and next steps

1. Add these as contextual research references with explicit evidence type and protocol fit. Keep research location separate from comparison eligibility.
2. Prioritize body-fat DXA references for a possible additional percentile comparator. Extract tables and methods, review subgroup coverage and permissions, and test known values before enabling it.
3. Balance has useful age references, but not permission to infer extreme percentiles from a small mean/SD table. Broad jump, 5 km and lifting studies support repeatable tests rather than worldwide ranks.
4. Specify Zone 2 operationally. Specify a recognized sleep regularity algorithm or rename the current manually entered wearable score. Specify simple/choice reaction time and a calibrated task.
5. Keep dips as personal tracking/challenge until the exact untimed protocol has suitable reliability/validation evidence; the located studies are partial matches.
6. Validate the precise 1RM estimation formula by lift, repetition range and load definition before presenting it as equivalent to a directly tested maximum.
7. Keep laboratory metrics clinically contextual. Percentile rarity, clinical thresholds and desirable intervention targets are three different things.
8. Research does not activate a source automatically: preserve the current three published-comparator configurations until the reviewed reference and metadata requirements are implemented.

No app scoring, numeric thresholds, database or production deployment changed during this research pass.
