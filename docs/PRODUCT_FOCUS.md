# TOP1% mobile training loop

## Product

Pick a measurable target and date, build a feasible weekly schedule, log what happened, and retest. The four main screens are Home, Measurements, Program and My profile. My profile contains history, rankings, the optional friends ladder, language and account settings. The measurement journal supports 36 active metrics. The program accepts the 16 physical performance tests in the strength catalog. Goals can use an absolute 1RM in kg when the original lift has a recorded bodyweight. New programs use the saved measurement as their baseline.

Home lets the athlete select among active goals, shows the selected forecast and next session, and offers a retest of the latest measurement when there is no goal. Quick log always offers the 5 km run, grip strength and squat in that order. The goal card also opens its own test. A recorded quick-log test opens a dated retest. On Home, a compatible published cohort selects a schematic curve showing the supported range. A challenge test selects the TOP1 pyramid. Other journal entries keep their measured units. IP-derived country from Vercel sets regional dates and the first day of the displayed week. Without a hosting hint the calendar starts on Monday. Device language and training time zone are independent of country. The separate favorite action can still be pinned in Profile.

## Training and scenarios

Program suggests next steps from saved performance measurements. The athlete can select multiple measured tests, set a target for each and choose from dates calculated for the available training visits. Named collections suggest related measured tests using available equipment and prior choices. A shared calendar divides the training budget across those goals. Athletes can add another goal or stop one. Recent-pace forecasts use comparable recorded responses and documented attendance. The [Programme evidence and validation plan](PROGRAMME_EVIDENCE_AND_VALIDATION.md) describes how TOP1 uses prior results and tests the forecasts. Guided plans generate dated sessions, working doses, recovery time and a final retest. Workout logs preserve the prescribed dose and the work actually completed. Existing manually configured programs retain their settings and history.

Progressive resistance training and repeated practice support the exercise choices. A small four-week trial found improved push-up progression after three sessions per week in moderately trained young men. The study measured progression through exercise variations. TOP1 supplies the specific working sets and progression. [ACSM resistance training guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) · [Kotarsky et al. trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466268/)

Guided Program estimates assume the athlete completes all remaining scheduled workouts. The day-one scenario assumes a 50% chance of a small gain per workout and displays the gain size. The sensitivity range uses 40% and 60% response assumptions. After three fully completed intervals between comparable dated tests, the model samples the athlete’s own gains, plateaus and declines. Recovery and retest days contribute zero training sessions. These simulations await validation against subsequent real-world goal outcomes.

A personal simulation becomes available after at least three trained intervals between four comparable, dated tests. Under the adaptive program, a completed workout must match its own recorded prescription. This allows planned progressions while excluding substitutions, unlogged slots, and intervals with incompatible test protocols. A same-day measured baseline can anchor the first interval when it matches the program’s starting value. It samples recorded intervals under compatible training conditions, including stagnation, declines and logged absences. The panel displays the interval count, completed sessions and remaining scheduled sessions. Its percentage is the share of simulated paths reaching the target by the deadline.

The editable scenario in previously configured manual programs works backward from the target and remaining scheduled sessions. It counts how many positive responses of an assumed size would close the gap and computes a binomial tail using completion probability and conditional response probability. On day one the example assumes 80% attendance, a 50% response chance when attending and a gain equal to about 1% of baseline, rounded to at least one model increment. These are editable scenario settings. A saved scenario is specific to the prescription. Changing the dose resets the displayed scenario to the illustrative defaults. The model assumes independent sessions and a fixed gain per effective session. Its output is conditional. Estimating a calibrated individual probability requires prospective outcomes for comparable goals, doses and horizons, with held-out evaluation. Preserve each forecast before its outcome and evaluate calibration by person and time, accounting for missing follow-up and subgroup coverage. Report uncertainty on an independent cohort before making a claim about success rates. [External-validation guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-074820)

The private `GET /api/performance-insights` endpoint computes both models from the signed-in user’s current measurements, test history and training ledger. Guest calculations use the same functions on the device. The goal model sends a hypothetical target result back through the four-area scoring function to calculate the overall TOP1 position at that target, holding the other measurements and test context fixed. For a target in kilograms the projection requires recorded test-day bodyweight. The prediction uses changes in the goal’s original units. A game score alone supplies no training response data.

## Checking goal forecasts against outcomes

Program keeps each saved goal target and deadline with the schedule that was in effect. A comparable test that reaches the target by the end of the deadline in the plan’s time zone establishes success. A comparable below-target test on that last day establishes a miss once the day ends. A late test is shown separately. An attempt without a qualifying retest has an unknown result. Replacing a goal preserves its earlier attempt. The workout breakdown distinguishes full prescribed sessions, partial sessions, explicit skips and unlogged slots. The attendance percentage uses sessions due through attainment, replacement or the deadline.

For a signed-in guided plan, TOP1 saves the first eligible forecast at each estimate stage with its actual issue time, unadjusted probability and issued probability. A new observed outcome can correct or withdraw a label. Guests can use private goal tracking on their device. Contributing to pooled estimates is a separate, optional Program setting. It covers future eligible forecasts and withdrawing removes membership of previous contributions. The account export includes the private forecast receipts.

Empirical adjustments compare mature goals with matching test, units, forecast version, estimate stage, time remaining, relative target gap and original probability band. Each other participant contributes at most one attempt. Both chronological groups must have measured follow-up for at least 70% of matching participants. Fitting and evaluation then require at least 20 independent participants per group with a measured outcome and full adherence to the sessions due after that specific forecast. Only outcomes available before the later group's forecasts can train the correction. TOP1 shrinks the observed calibration error toward zero, caps the adjustment at 15 percentage points, and applies it only if it improves mean squared probability error (Brier score) by at least 0.002 in that later group. These thresholds are deployment choices. They do not establish general accuracy for every athlete. Missing tests, self-reported workouts and voluntary participation limit what these results can establish.

## Comparing performance

Published comparisons require a matching test, protocol and cohort and report the precision supported by the source. The 13 open tests have individual TOP1 game scales and a displayed upper bound of Top 0.01% or better. The provisional overall percentage updates with the visible area count after each performance test. [Rules by test](PERFORMANCE_GAME_RULES.md) All 36 metrics support an empirical personal comparison after repeated entries. Dated, comparable tests take priority. A comparison of quick entries describes the numerical position among the user's logged values. A high numeric percentile for a lab or recovery measurement has no implied health benefit.

The rank visualization names its comparison group. Its schematic curve does not estimate the observed shape of a population. The history view preserves recorded values, dates and provenance.
